Power transformers are among the most important and expensive pieces of equipment in an electrical network. A transformer failure can result in costly repairs, extended downtime, and interruptions to power supply.

One of the electrical stresses that transformers must withstand is transient overvoltage.

## How Transformers Can Be Exposed to Surges

A transient can enter a transformer through an incoming overhead line, cable connection, or switching operation elsewhere in the network.

Lightning-related surges are particularly important in outdoor electrical systems. Even when a lightning strike does not occur directly at a substation, the resulting traveling wave can propagate through the network.

Switching operations can create another type of transient. Circuit breakers and other switching devices can generate temporary overvoltages depending on the characteristics of the network and equipment being switched.

## What Does a Surge Arrester Do?

A surge arrester is designed to limit excessive voltage and divert surge current to ground.

During normal system operation, it does not continuously conduct significant current. When the voltage rises to a level associated with a transient event, the arrester becomes conductive and helps keep the voltage within a level that the protected insulation can withstand.

This is particularly useful around transformer terminals.

## Choosing the Protection Level

Transformer protection should be considered together with insulation coordination. The arrester's protection level needs to be appropriate for the transformer's insulation withstand capability and the characteristics of the electrical system.

Important considerations can include:

* Rated system voltage
* Continuous operating voltage
* Transformer insulation level
* Grounding configuration
* Lightning exposure
* Switching surge conditions
* Arrester energy capability
* Installation distance from the transformer

The physical connection also matters. Long connections between the arrester and transformer can affect the voltage actually seen by the transformer during a fast transient.

## Beyond the Transformer

Although transformers are an important application, surge protection should not be considered only at the transformer itself. Incoming lines, switchgear, busbars, and other connected equipment may also require protection depending on the network design.

This is why surge protection is normally evaluated as part of the overall substation or distribution system.
