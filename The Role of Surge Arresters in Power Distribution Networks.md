Power distribution networks are exposed to a variety of electrical disturbances. Outdoor overhead lines are particularly vulnerable to lightning activity, while switching operations can also generate transient overvoltages.

Surge arresters provide one method of limiting these temporary voltage stresses.

Protecting Distribution Equipment

Distribution transformers, switchgear, cables, and other electrical equipment can be affected by transient overvoltages.

A properly selected surge arrester helps limit the voltage that reaches connected equipment and can reduce the risk of insulation damage.

Metal Oxide Technology

Modern surge arresters commonly use metal oxide, particularly zinc oxide, as the active protection element.

The nonlinear voltage-current characteristics of zinc oxide allow the arrester to remain relatively non-conductive under normal conditions while conducting surge current when the voltage rises significantly.

Outdoor Distribution Applications

Surge arresters are commonly used on outdoor distribution equipment and in substations.

For example, a 33 kV metal oxide lightning arrester can be configured for compatible outdoor power systems, with the exact electrical characteristics selected according to the project requirements.

Selection Should Be Application-Based

The correct arrester depends on the system voltage, grounding arrangement, equipment insulation level, installation environment, and required protection characteristics.

It is therefore better to evaluate the complete application rather than select a product based on voltage alone.
