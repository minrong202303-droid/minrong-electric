
Power networks must continue operating safely even when faults, equipment failures, lightning events, or switching disturbances occur.

Substations help manage these conditions by combining protection, switching, monitoring, and voltage transformation equipment.

## Fault Detection and Interruption

Electrical faults can cause very high currents that damage conductors, transformers, and other equipment.

Protection relays monitor electrical conditions and identify abnormal situations. When a fault is detected, the relay can send a trip signal to a circuit breaker.

The circuit breaker then interrupts the fault current and isolates the affected part of the network.

## Electrical Isolation

After a circuit has been disconnected, disconnectors can be used to provide visible isolation.

This is important during inspection, repair, and maintenance work. It allows personnel to identify which section has been separated from the energized system.

Disconnectors and circuit breakers therefore serve different but complementary functions.

## Protection Against Transient Overvoltages

Not all electrical disturbances are high-current faults.

Lightning and switching operations can create transient overvoltages that stress equipment insulation. Surge arresters help limit these voltage surges and direct the associated current toward ground.

They are commonly considered for transformer terminals, incoming lines, and other exposed parts of a substation.

## Measurement and Control

Current transformers and voltage transformers provide measurement signals for protection, metering, and control systems.

These signals help operators and automated systems understand network conditions and respond to abnormal events.

Modern substations may also use monitoring and communication systems to improve operational awareness.

## Coordinated Protection

Protection equipment should not be selected separately from the rest of the substation.

The protection scheme must be compatible with the network configuration, fault levels, transformer characteristics, insulation requirements, and switching arrangement.

Coordination helps ensure that the affected section is isolated while unnecessary interruptions to other parts of the network are minimized.
