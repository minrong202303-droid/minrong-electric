Voltage transformers are essential components in electrical measurement and protection systems. They reduce high primary voltage to a standardized lower secondary voltage that can be used by meters, monitoring devices, and protective relays.

Although voltage ratio is often the first parameter considered during selection, it is not the only one that determines whether a voltage transformer is suitable for an application.

Accuracy class, secondary burden, insulation level, and installation conditions are equally important.

How Does a Voltage Transformer Work?

A voltage transformer, also known as a potential transformer (PT), provides an electrically isolated secondary voltage signal proportional to the primary system voltage.

For example, an indoor switchgear installation may use a voltage transformer to supply signals to:

Voltage measuring instruments

Energy meters

Protection relays

Power monitoring systems

Control and indication circuits

The secondary signal allows connected equipment to monitor the electrical system without being directly connected to the high-voltage primary circuit.

Voltage transformers are therefore important for both operational monitoring and electrical protection.

1. Match the Primary Voltage to the System

The primary rated voltage must correspond to the electrical system and the intended connection arrangement.

For indoor distribution switchgear, common voltage transformer configurations include 3kV, 6kV, and 10kV classes.

Engineers should verify the system voltage, grounding arrangement, phase connection, and insulation requirements before selecting a model.

A voltage ratio that looks correct on paper may still be unsuitable if the transformer is not designed for the actual system configuration.

2. Choose the Correct Accuracy Class

Accuracy class describes the permitted measurement error under specified operating conditions.

Different applications have different requirements.

For example:

Measurement circuits may require a defined measurement accuracy class.

Monitoring systems may have different accuracy requirements from billing metering.

Protection circuits must be evaluated according to the requirements of the connected protection equipment.

A higher accuracy class is not automatically necessary for every application. The correct choice depends on the function of the secondary circuit and the applicable project standards.

3. Calculate the Secondary Burden

Secondary burden is one of the most important factors in voltage transformer selection.

It represents the total load connected to the transformer secondary, usually expressed in volt-amperes (VA).

The connected burden may include:

Voltage meters

Protection relays

Monitoring instruments

Control devices

Secondary wiring losses

If the total burden exceeds the transformer's rated output for the required accuracy class, the measurement performance may no longer meet the specified accuracy requirements.

Engineers should calculate the expected connected burden and compare it with the transformer's rated output.

It is also important to distinguish between rated output at a particular accuracy class and limiting output. These values are not interchangeable.

4. Consider Insulation and Installation Conditions

Indoor voltage transformers are commonly installed inside switchgear assemblies.

Their insulation design must match the system voltage and required insulation level. Engineers should also consider installation dimensions, terminal arrangements, operating environment, and compatibility with the switchgear structure.

Epoxy cast resin insulation is commonly used in indoor instrument transformers because it provides an insulating structure suitable for enclosed electrical equipment.

The final selection should be based on the manufacturer's technical drawings and project requirements.

5. Review Applicable Standards and Documentation

Before purchasing a voltage transformer, buyers should request technical documents covering:

Rated primary and secondary voltage

Accuracy classes

Rated output and limiting output

Insulation level

Connection diagram

Dimensions and installation details

Applicable standards and available compliance documents

IEC 61869-3 is an important reference standard for inductive voltage transformers. Buyers should confirm the applicable edition and the specific documentation available for the proposed model.

Conclusion

Voltage transformer selection should be based on the complete secondary circuit rather than the voltage ratio alone.

Matching the primary voltage, accuracy class, secondary burden, insulation level, and installation requirements helps ensure that measurement and protection systems receive suitable voltage signals.

For project procurement, providing the system voltage, required accuracy class, connected burden, and installation details will make technical evaluation more accurate.
