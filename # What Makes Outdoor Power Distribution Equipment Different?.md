
Outdoor power distribution equipment operates in an environment that is considerably less controlled than an indoor electrical room. Rain, dust, temperature changes, sunlight, humidity, and pollution can all influence equipment performance.

This is why outdoor electrical equipment requires careful consideration during both product selection and system design.

## Environmental Conditions

The local environment should be evaluated before selecting outdoor equipment.

Temperature range, altitude, humidity, pollution level, wind conditions, and exposure to rain can all affect the required equipment design.

In coastal or heavily polluted areas, for example, insulation performance may require particular attention.

## Mechanical Construction

Outdoor switching equipment needs to withstand environmental exposure while maintaining reliable mechanical operation.

The supporting structure, operating mechanism, enclosures where applicable, and insulation components all need to be suitable for the installation environment.

## Electrical Performance

The basic electrical requirements remain just as important as they are for indoor equipment.

Rated voltage, rated current, insulation level, short-time withstand current, and fault conditions should be matched to the electrical system.

For switching equipment, engineers should also consider whether the device is intended for load switching, fault interruption, isolation, or another specific function.

## Maintenance and Accessibility

Outdoor equipment should be positioned so that inspection and maintenance can be performed safely.

Good accessibility can reduce maintenance difficulties over the equipment's service life.

## Selecting the Right Equipment

There is no single outdoor electrical product suitable for every power distribution project. Equipment should be selected according to the electrical system, environmental conditions, installation arrangement, and applicable technical requirements.
