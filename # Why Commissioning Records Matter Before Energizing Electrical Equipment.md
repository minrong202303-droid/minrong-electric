
Installing electrical equipment is only one part of completing a power distribution project. Before energization, project teams also need to confirm that the equipment has been installed correctly, inspected properly and tested in accordance with the approved project requirements.

Commissioning records provide evidence of these checks. They also create a useful reference for future operation, troubleshooting and maintenance.

## What Should Commissioning Records Include?

The exact documentation depends on the equipment type, project specification and applicable standards. However, several categories are commonly important.

### 1. Equipment Identification

Records should clearly identify the equipment being inspected or tested.

Typical information includes:

- Equipment name and model
- Serial number or identification code
- Rated voltage and current
- Installation location
- Manufacturer information
- Relevant drawings and document references

Accurate identification helps prevent test results from being assigned to the wrong equipment.

### 2. Visual and Mechanical Inspection

Before electrical testing, the project team should verify the physical installation.

Depending on the equipment, checks may cover:

- Foundation and mounting conditions
- Electrical clearances and connections
- Earthing connections
- Mechanical interlocks
- Operating mechanism condition
- Cable termination and enclosure condition

Any deviation from approved drawings or manufacturer instructions should be reviewed and resolved before proceeding.

### 3. Electrical Test Results

Electrical tests should be selected according to the equipment type and approved commissioning procedure.

For example, a circuit breaker may require checks of contact resistance, insulation condition, mechanical operation and relevant timing characteristics. Instrument transformers, cables and surge protection devices require different test methods.

Test records should identify the test method, equipment used, measured results, acceptance criteria and responsible personnel.

It is important not to apply one generic acceptance limit to every product. The applicable standard, manufacturer's documentation and project specification should determine the criteria.

### 4. Protection and Control Verification

Primary equipment cannot operate reliably without correctly configured control and protection circuits.

Depending on the system, commissioning may include verification of:

- Control wiring and terminal connections
- Trip and close circuits
- Interlocking logic
- Protection relay settings
- Alarm and indication circuits
- Remote and local control functions

Functional checks help confirm that the equipment responds correctly to the intended commands and protection signals.

## Why Documentation Matters After Energization

Commissioning records are not only required for project handover. They also establish a baseline for future maintenance.

If abnormal test results appear several years later, engineers can compare them with the original commissioning data. This comparison may help identify changes in contact resistance, insulation condition, operating time or other measurable parameters.

Good records also support warranty discussions, maintenance planning and technical audits.

## Avoid Treating Commissioning as a Paperwork Exercise

A complete document package does not automatically prove that equipment is ready for service. Test results must be reviewed by qualified personnel, deviations must be resolved, and the final energization decision must follow the project's safety and operating procedures.

The value of commissioning documentation comes from the relationship between the recorded results and the actual condition of the installed equipment.
